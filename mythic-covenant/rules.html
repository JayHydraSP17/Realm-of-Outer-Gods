Rulings at a Glance

The calls that come up every fight, with the math done for you. Full rules are in the chapters below; where this page and a chapter disagree, the chapter wins.

Knockback

Any damage can push the target unless you choose to keep it in place. Distance = base for the damage type + your STR or DEX mod + 5 ft per 10 ft you moved before the hit. Distance left over when it hits something is what hurts.

  • Piercing has no knockback unless something augments it. Teleporting and forced movement don't count as moving for the bonus.
  • Into a wall, door or barricade: 1d8 bludgeoning per 5 ft of unused knockback, and Stunned until the end of its next turn (reinforced surfaces may bounce it instead).
  • Into another creature: both take twice their own hit die, and both make a DEX save or fall Prone.
  • 20 ft or more can crack ice, shatter glass, or break weak cover. Off a ledge, it falls (see Falling).

Launching

Move at least 10 ft toward the target (or use a Launcher ability), hit, and beat 10 + (its CON mod × 2) with the attack roll.

  • Airborne: −4 AC, takes 1.5× damage, can't use reactions, lands Prone unless it can fly or saves.
  • Juggle: up to 3 hits before it lands. The last can be a Critical Air Takedown: +2 weapon dice, an extra status, and a slam into terrain if positioned.
  • Flyers make a DEX save to right themselves. Disabled wings auto-fail. Bosses and elites are often immune until legs, wings or back are disabled.

Falling

The handbook uses the standard falling rule: 1d6 bludgeoning per 10 ft fallen (max 20d6), and the creature lands Prone. Slow Fall and similar features reduce it.

  • Knocked or launched off a ledge: resolve knockback first, then the fall from where it ends up.
  • Pitfalls as an environmental takedown: Falling + Prone, and it counts as a Critical Takedown.

Limb targeting

Declare the limb before rolling; the target's AC goes up by the limb's modifier. A hit deals normal damage plus the effect.

Layer disables: wings + legs = no movement; add the spine for full immobility. Multiple disables open legendary foes to executions.

Takedowns

Needs: the target is Prone or Airborne, or Stunned, Grappled, Restrained or Vulnerable (Vulnerable ones must also be Prone or Airborne); you're in melee range. Costs 1 Dynamic Reaction or 3 Momentum. The damage can't be healed this combat and ignores resistance.

Critical Takedowns: environmental hazards, a 6-Momentum takedown, the end of a juggle, or a feature that says so. Bosses can't be Stunned: they Stagger instead (−1 Dynamic Reaction, −1 attack, no casting for a turn).

Reactions & feints

  • 3 Dynamic Reactions a round for every special-or-higher creature (grunts get 1); they refresh at the start of your turn. At 0 you're Vulnerable and open to Takedowns.
  • Declare first: the defender declares a reaction before the attack is rolled.
  • Undodgeable attacks: a Dodge lets it through at +10% damage. Unblockable: a Block lets it through at +10%.
  • Feints cost Tempo. A reaction the feint baits makes the defender Vulnerable to your next real attack; any other reaction declared against it is still spent. See Reading a Feint for the read check and feint fatigue.

Creating Your Character

There are a few things to note before creating your character:

  • MUST be Human.
  • Choose Noble vs. Commoner. There are many social differences between the two, so choose wisely.
  • Nobles get +2 to Charisma AND (Intelligence OR Wisdom).
  • Commoners get +2 to Constitution AND (Strength OR Dexterity).
  • Start at Level 3 and choose one 5e feat (must be approved by the DM).
  • Choose a god whom you worship.
  • Homebrew classes and subclasses must be approved by the DM.
  • Gestalt Leveling is unlocked (see below).

Max Level: 50

Characters now progress all the way to level 50, matching the five equipment tiers (Forged 1–10, Tempered 11–20, Masterwork 21–30, Mythic 31–40, Transcendent 41–50). With each level gained, power expands not linearly but exponentially. Players will scale far beyond traditional limits, capable of clashing with armies, titans, and world-ending threats.

Gestalt Leveling

Unlocked. Characters level in two classes simultaneously. Each level gained applies to both classes at once. This allows hybrid growth: martial/caster builds, tank/controller mixes, and more. Balancing limits:

  • Stacking Features: Most features from both classes apply. Identical features (such as Unarmored Defense) do not stack; only the stronger one applies. Extra Attacks do stack from gestalt leveling.
  • Spellcasting: If both classes cast spells, take the higher of the two.
  • Health Gain: On level-up, gain both hit dice, adding both together and adding your Constitution twice.

Attribute Gains

Every two levels, players gain +4 attribute points to distribute freely. At intervals of 10 levels, gain a free feat as well.

  • Attributes can go beyond 20. The soft cap is 100 for late-game campaigns and special scenarios. Effects scaling off ability scores continue to improve, though diminishing returns apply at extremely high levels.

Maximum Output Philosophy

The system operates under the principle of "Maximized Impact." Damage, healing, HP, and resistances use maximum dice values; we never roll damage. This keeps high-power scenarios clear and fast.

Custom Feats

In addition to standard feats, the DM may grant Custom Feats. These are narrative or mechanically driven boons unique to the character's choices, backstory, or actions. Some allow minor rule rewrites; others may change how a class feature functions. Custom feats are a core pillar of personalization and are treated as earned evolution.

New Classes and Class Changes

The core classes of D\&D were built for a different kind of world, a world with clean lines, clear good and evil, and simple problems. But Genbu is a broken place, filled with forgotten gods, failed utopias, corrupted rulers, and a battlefield that never stops shifting. Below are new classes created for those who walk its ruined highways. These classes have been tested to synergize with the combat chapters, including Momentum, Dynamic Reactions, and Status Mixing.

  • The Vagabond — An alternative to the fighter, using desperation and maneuvers to deal devastating damage.
  • The Spell Blade — A martial arcane hybrid that fuses weapon work with spells, with powerful aegis to tailor your playstyle.
  • The Talent — A psionic warrior using ESP to deliver supernatural attacks and buffs.
  • The Craftsman — Craft dangerous inventions to aid in combat and RP.
  • The Warden — An elemental tank that uses aspects of the natural world to soak damage and debuff enemies.
  • The Favored Soul — Born with a spark of a god's power, or blessed by one. An intersection of Paladin and Warlock.
  • The Inscriptor — A pact caster who writes magic into existence, rewarded by patrons who act as editors and muses.
  • The Petal Knight — Arcane duelists whose magic manifests as a flower embodying their soul. Mid-range support duelists.
  • The Sword Saint — Samurai-like front-liners; nova-round fighters who rely on resource management.
  • The Witch — A control caster using hexes to help or hinder.
  • Summoner — A Wisdom-based half-caster with a customizable summon.
  • The Binder — A jack of all trades, using spirits to gain new proficiencies and features.
  • Revised Monk — Buffed significantly into a stronger DPS with exclusive martial arts weapons and 2024 monk changes.
  • Revised Ranger — d12 hit die, a much better Hunter's Mark, an initiative-changing arrow, and Debilitating Arrows.

Life at Palmora

Palmora, the School of the Celestially Gifted, runs on a calendar, a timetable, and a faculty with their own agendas. This chapter covers how days pass, how classes and grades work, who teaches you, and what you can do with your free time.

Calendar System

The campaign follows the Roman Calendar from 1615 onward. Each day is unique, so players must manage their time wisely to progress their characters and the story.

Timed Events & Deadlines

Timed Events are enacted by the DM; most are known to the players, but the DM may keep some hidden. Deadlines are requests given to players by NPCs. They are often prerequisites for Support Conversations, and each provides a different resource for later. Keep track of requests unless you wish to plummet through the domain of your gods.

Weekly Cycle

At the DM's discretion the party fast-forwards to the weekend (Saturday) and chooses one of three options:

  • Explore: enjoy the weekend at school with all of its facilities.
  • Attack: choose a mission from the board and see what unfolds.
  • Shop: travel to Danu, the merchant district, to buy resources and explore the Greater Atlas.

Special events can interrupt a cycle. Servant skills that require time spent together must be declared to the DM before the cycle.

Automated Progression

Since Mythic Covenant is heavy in relationship building, resource gathering, and story progression, some progress is handled automatically between sessions so the DM can keep the main story moving.

Relationships & Allies

Bonds are power in Mythic Covenant. Relationships unlock conversations, combat bonuses, team-ups, and eventually recruits who fight under your command.

Relationships Matter

NPCs in Mythic Covenant are more than background figures or quest-givers. They have lives, goals, and feelings of their own. How you interact with them shapes your story and the world around you.

Each important NPC has a Relationship Tier with your character, tracked from F (distrust/indifference) to S (deep bond). This tier affects how much an NPC expresses themselves, what they're willing to do for you, and how their personal storylines unfold. Relationships give bonuses in combat (see Relationships & Allies) and in roleplay: higher tiers influence an NPC giving you requests, accepting your commands, and helping you learn about the world.

DM Discretion: Only one S-Tier Bond can exist between two characters at a time. It represents a lifelong connection, such as a sibling bond, best friendship, or marriage.

Each tier has a visible pip threshold, so progress isn't random. Once the threshold is reached you can have a Support Conversation to raise the tier. Some tiers are locked by story progress.

Unit Relationship Charts

Units have their own relationship charts with each other, not just with you. Recruited allies and NPC units gain relationship benefits in both Active Supports and Linked Supports. Pair allies who actually get along.

Godly Oversight

How are you supposed to know what your god likes if they don't tell you? One way is praying. But while you interact with the world, your god may also send a note about their likes or dislikes in the current situation, for example "Orochius is displeased by your truth-telling" or "Necrolien is pleased with the number of souls added to his collection." These notes are sent privately and are different from praying. A god's favor can also raise or lower your Transformation Limit (TL).

Allies & Supports

Unit Limit

At the start of every combat the DM sets how many active units may participate. It's influenced by Team Combat participation, the map, and the situation (low for stealth, high for full battles). Choose allies wisely and swap them to test synergies.

Active Supports

An ally fighting within the Unit Limit, using their full abilities. When within 5 ft of a character they have a relationship with, both gain:

  • Can't die unless the situation demands it (e.g. a boss fight).

Combat Fundamentals

Mythic Covenant's combat systems align with J.T.'s Improved DND. Where the two differ, this handbook is the rule for this campaign. Combat is movement-focused, reaction-driven, and highly lethal: positioning matters, momentum is rewarded, the battlefield is a weapon, and enemies will dodge, block, counter, and fake. So should you.

Key Terms

  • Momentum Stacks — Earned through movement; boost damage and can be spent for extra attacks.
  • Vulnerable — A creature that cannot take any more reactions (out of stamina). It is extremely unsafe and can be targeted by a Takedown. If a takedown is performed on a Vulnerable creature, it regains 1 Dynamic Reaction until the next round. This is different from 5e damage vulnerability.
  • Dynamic Reaction — Special responses to enemy behavior (Block, Dodge, Parry, Riposte, etc.).
  • Tempo — The resource formerly called Feint Tokens. Spent on Feints and Deflects.
  • Undodgeable attack — Dodging it lets the hit through with +10% damage.
  • Unblockable attack — Blocking it lets the hit through with +10% damage.
  • Execution — A takedown that kills, granting bonus healing.

Knockback

Whenever you deal damage, you can force the target to move in a direction, unless you choose to keep it in place.

Damage Type Base Knockback Notes
Bludgeoning 15 ft + STR or DEX mod Most common knockback
Slashing 10 ft + mod Causes Bleed
Piercing 0 ft No knockback unless augmented; causes Bleed
Force 20 ft + mod Highest knockback
  • Momentum augments distance: for every 10 ft moved before the hit, add 5 ft of knockback.
  • Large knockbacks (20 ft+) can crack ice, shatter glass, or break weak cover.

Environmental Interactions

1. Solid surfaces (walls, doors, barricades): 1d8 bludgeoning per 5 ft of unused knockback, and Stunned until the end of its next turn. Reinforced surfaces may bounce the creature instead (DM discretion).

2. Other creatures: both take damage equal to twice their respective hit die, and both make a DEX save (DM sets DC) or fall Prone.

3. Destructible objects (furniture, fences, crates): 1d10–10d10 slashing, piercing, or bludgeoning, with optional Bleed, Blind (dust), or cover collapse. May open new paths or hazards.

4. Hazards (fires, spikes, machinery): full hazard damage (often 2× base) plus listed statuses. Hazards may react: fire spreads, machinery jams or explodes, alarms trigger.

5. Terrain collapse or traps: fragile supports can collapse terrain, cause falling damage, or open new elevation. STR or DEX saves to avoid being trapped or buried.

6. Improvised use: use Momentum, grapples, or extra attacks to shove enemies into ceilings (launch + knockback), off cliffs, onto slick terrain, or into magical runes.

GM Note: Reward players who think creatively. Let them experiment.

Momentum

Momentum is a kinetic advantage gained by moving before you strike.

  • Gain +1 weapon damage die per 10 ft moved in a straight line before your attack (unless a feat modifies it), using the weapon's die type.
  • Maximum +6 damage dice per attack, unless modified by feats or features.
  • Applies to dashing, sprinting, jumping, climbing, and wall running. Teleportation and forced movement don't count.
  • Only physical attacks benefit unless an ability says otherwise. Momentum is optional; you may skip knockback to keep an enemy close.
Stacks Effect
1–2 Bonus damage; knockback can be used to launch.
3 Spend all current stacks to make 1 extra attack.
6 Momentum Finisher — auto-critical and applies a weapon-based status (Bleed, Shock, etc.).

You gain 1 stack each time you move at least 10 ft toward an attack (max 6). Momentum resets if you are hit, grappled, or knocked prone; you end your turn without a successful attack; you attack without moving 10 ft first; or your turn ends.

Launching

To launch a target vertically: move at least 10 ft toward it (or have a Launcher ability), hit, and have the attack roll exceed 10 + (CON mod × 2). An airborne target:

  • suffers −4 AC and takes 1.5× damage;
  • cannot use reactions;
  • lands Prone unless it can fly or saves.

Juggling: up to 3 hits midair before it lands. The final hit may be a Critical Air Takedown: +2 weapon dice, an extra status effect, and a slam into terrain if positioned.

Flying enemies make a DEX save to right themselves and stay aloft. Disabled wings auto-fail. Bosses and elites are often immune unless legs, wings, or back are disabled.

Reactions & Feinting

Combat is a dialogue. Every special-or-higher creature reacts, baits, and punishes, and the order in which those choices are declared matters as much as the dice.

Dynamic Reactions

Replacing normal reactions, every special-or-higher creature, PC or NPC, has 3 Dynamic Reactions per round. Grunts and mooks have 1. They refresh at the start of your turn.

The Declare-First Rule

Players were waiting to see whether an attack hit before choosing to react ("Does he hit? Yes? OK, I dodge"). That made Dodge the only answer and Feints pointless. Every reaction window now resolves in this order:

1. Commit. The attacker declares the attack and secretly commits to whether it is real or a Feint (and which Feint or which branch of a technique with branches).

2. Read. If the defender intends to react, they may make a Read: an Insight check against the attacker's Feint DC. The DM rolls Reads for NPCs. A defender who isn't going to react, or has no Dynamic Reactions left, skips this step.

3. Declare. The defender declares their reaction, or holds, before any die is read aloud.

4. Resolve.

  • Covered: Block, Dodge, Parry, and Special Reactions.
  • Not covered: reactions with conditional triggers ("on their miss," "on their hit," "an ally fails a save"), such as Riposte, Help, and Team Attack, keep their normal timing.
  • Special Reactions with their own trigger (e.g. "when targeted") keep it and resolve before the attack.

The result is rock-paper-scissors: Block is the safe read, Dodge is the gamble, and Parry is the last-ditch contest. The Read decides how much the defender knows before they throw.

Reaction Cost Trigger Effect Failure
Block 1 Dynamic Reaction Declared vs. incoming physical attack Reduces damage. The ladder only advances when you are actually hit: 100% → 75% → 50% → 25% → Vulnerable. Read: while Blocking, you have advantage on your next Read against that attacker. Ladder advances on each hit taken while blocking.
Dodge 1 Dynamic Reaction Declared vs. incoming attack, AoE, or spell DEX save vs. DC 8 + the attacker's attack modifier. On a success, the attack is negated. Requires 5 ft of open space. The save is made even if the attack would have missed. Take full damage or effects and fall Prone.
Deflect 1 Tempo Block is active, incoming melee attack Cancel the hit and steal the attacker's turn; you act immediately. If the Deflect was baited, you become Vulnerable.
Parry (Monk only) 1 Dynamic Reaction Declared vs. a weapon attack Both creatures roll attacks; the higher wins. On a win, negate the hit and strike back. Reaction wasted and the attack goes through.
Riposte 1 Dynamic Reaction Enemy misses a melee attack Immediately counter with an attack action (no bonus action; War Caster can modify). If you miss, you become Vulnerable.
Help (Save) 1 Dynamic Reaction Ally within 15 ft fails a save Use your save instead of theirs; both succeed or both fail. If you fail, both suffer the full effect.
Team Attack 1 Dynamic Reaction Ally attacks a shared target Attack at the same time for combo damage. If either attack misses, both are Vulnerable until the next round.

Block + ADR stacking under playtest: with ADR, Block reduction, and Reads, Block may be too strong. No change is applied yet; the fix will be chosen after testing before the Equinox battle.

Tempo & Feinting

Feint Tokens are now called Tempo. You have Tempo equal to your Proficiency Bonus, refreshed on a short rest. Tempo is spent on Feints and Deflects, and will expand to other tactical uses later. Abilities that granted Feint Tokens now grant Tempo.

Feints are deliberate deceptions that bait a specific reaction and punish the enemy for falling for it. If the opponent reacts incorrectly, their reaction is wasted and they become Vulnerable to your next real attack. If they don't react, nothing happens, but now they're guessing. Bosses and intelligent enemies can Feint too.

Which reactions count as falling for it: any reaction declared against a Feint is spent. Only a reaction the Feint baits (listed below) makes the defender Vulnerable. For example, Blocking an Undodgeable Feint wastes the reaction but doesn't make the defender Vulnerable.

Feint Timing

Feints are split by timing so they no longer drain your reaction pool on your own turn:

Reactive Feint The enemy's turn 1 Dynamic Reaction + 1 Tempo Guard Feint, Dodge Feint

Feint Baits Out Description
Undodgeable Feint Dodge, movement-based reactions You wind up a sweeping blow, forcing evasive action, but never swing.

Hidden commitment: the attacker secretly commits to real or Feint before the defender declares. For NPCs, the DM locks in the NPC's response before the attacker reveals (or rolls a d4). A defender only learns the commit through a successful Read. When an NPC defends, the DM rolls its Read; on a success the player reveals to the DM before the NPC declares. On a failure, the DM picks the NPC's response from its temperament (below) and locks it in before the player reveals.

Feints + Limb Targeting: feinting a headshot then swinging low at the knee is valid and encouraged.

Takedowns & Executions

Takedowns are cinematic finishers that reward positioning, status application, and momentum. Requirements:

  • Conditioned: the enemy is Prone or Airborne, or Stunned, Grappled, Restrained, or Vulnerable. Vulnerable creatures must also be Prone or Airborne.
  • Positioned: you are within melee range.
  • Cost: 1 Dynamic Reaction or 3 Momentum stacks.

Takedown damage is non-regenerable (can't be healed except by specific mythic-class effects; it lowers max HP for the combat) and bypasses normal resistance, but not immunity unless it's a Critical Takedown.

Target Takedown Critical Takedown
Grunts / Minions Instant kill Instant kill
Special Enemies 25% max HP 50% max HP
Bosses 10% max HP 20% max HP

A Critical Takedown happens when you use an Environmental Hazard, a 6-stack Momentum takedown, a qualifying feat or class feature, or a takedown at the end of a juggle.

Stagger (boss rule): bosses can't be Stunned. Any stun instead Staggers: −1 Dynamic Reaction, −1 attack, no casting or ability activation for 1 turn. Can combo into a takedown if also Prone or Airborne.

Environmental Takedown Status Applied

Executions

When a takedown kills, it becomes an Execution:

Regular 15% max HP —
Critical 20% max HP 10% max HP

The healing is instantaneous and can stabilize otherwise doomed characters. Be creative: the DM may grant extra healing for a good one.

Dynamic Entrance (Optional)

Throwing or knocking a creature into a key feature (bridge, glass floor, elevator cable) can shift the battlefield: new hazards, a new stage, a second phase, split groups, or altered initiative and positions.

Final notes: takedowns can bypass enemy resistances; some elite and mythic foes may counter a failed takedown if not sufficiently debilitated.

Dangerous Attacks

Dangerous Attacks are cinematic turning points. They skip the attack roll and trigger automatically at the start of the next round. Targets must succeed on a series of saves (based on the maneuver's tier); failing all of them lands the full effect, and partial saves reduce it. They deal Mortal Damage, which permanently reduces max HP and can't be healed, and often cause limb injuries, statuses, or environmental moments.

Downed Teammate Options

  • Whimper: one short sentence on your turn, for free.
  • Help: grant the Help option; it costs one of your death saving throws.
  • Last Stand: sacrifice all your death saves for one final attack at 4× damage that automatically crits.
  • Final Spell: spellcasters may cast a spell 2 ranks above their limit (max 5th → 7th) at 2× effect with more crippling effects.

These are last-ditch efforts. You will die.

  • Medium (the full range at a measured level): +½ PB (round down) to Dodge, Block, and Deflect. Adaptation: once per round, when a defensive reaction succeeds, gain 1 Tempo.
  • Heavy (turns the wearer into a wall): −PB to Dodge. +PB to Block, and once per round the first Block doesn't cost a reaction. No bonus to Deflect.

Unarmored Defense

You choose either Unarmored Defense or armor. They don't stack, and Unarmored Defense gets no ADR or Reaction Synergy. New formula: 10 + Tier Bonus + stat investment, capped by tier:

Tier 1 2 3 4 5
UD Max AC 20 30 40 50 60

In-combat AC boosts (spells, class features) may exceed the cap. Overflow Toughness: for every 2 points your formula exceeds the cap (spells excluded), gain temp HP equal to one roll of the hit die of the class that grants Unarmored Defense, calculated at the start of combat. Example: a level 12 Monk (d8) calculates AC 34 against a Tier 2 cap of 30. Overflow 4 = 2d8 temp HP.

Barbarian ruling: Barbarian Unarmored Defense uses STR and CON instead of DEX and CON.

Armor Properties

Properties are universal by category. Light armor selects 3, Medium 2, Heavy 2; no duplicates. Scaling: Tier 1–2 use PB, Tier 3–4 use PS, Tier 5 gains the Tier 5 effect. Percentages are used only where they handle burst, repeated hits, takedown or percent damage better than flat numbers: Light uses them least, Heavy most.

Light Armor Properties

Built for offensive Dexterity characters who rely on movement, timing, feints, deflects, and precision.

Slipstream

You are hardest to catch while in motion.

  • Tier 1–2: If you moved at least 10 feet this turn, the first attack against you each round takes a penalty equal to PB
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If that attack misses, you may immediately reposition 10 feet without provoking, and the attacker cannot use offensive reactions against you this turn

Deadeye

You make called shots matter.

  • Tier 1–2: Your first called shot each round gains +PB
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If that called shot succeeds, choose one: the target loses 1 Dynamic Reaction, loses 1 Momentum stack, or takes a penalty equal to PS on its next Disable save or defensive contest

Trickster

Your Feints force bad decisions.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB to your Feint DC
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If a creature falls for your Feint, your next attack this turn cannot be Deflected, or it ignores part of the called-shot penalty, your choice

Counterstep

A missed attack gives you a better angle.

  • Tier 1–2: When a creature misses you in melee, gain +PB to your next attack against it
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If that attack hits, you may either move 5 feet for free, strip 1 Dynamic Reaction from the target, or reduce its next attack roll by PS

Perfect Deflect

A successful Deflect turns defense into immediate pressure.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB to Deflect contests
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: After a successful Deflect, your next attack this round gains +PS, cannot be blocked, and if it hits the target, cannot Deflect before the end of its next turn

Pursuit

Distance does not buy safety.

  • Tier 1–2: After you hit a creature, you may move 5 feet
  • Tier 3–4: Move 10 feet instead, and this movement does not provoke
  • Tier 5: If you end this movement outside the target’s reach, that target cannot use opportunity attacks or anti-movement reactions until the start of its next turn

Airborne

You thrive when the fight leaves the ground.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB against launch effects and airborne recovery checks
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: Once per round, if you recover while airborne, you may immediately make a basic attack or move up to half your speed before landing. Against airborne targets, your attacks gain +PS

Flowstate

Movement sharpens your offense without becoming a Momentum exploit engine.

  • Tier 1–2: If you move at least 10 feet before attacking, gain +PB to that attack
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If the attack hits, gain 1 Momentum stack once per round, and Momentum does not fully decay from one failed attack this round

Ghostline

You attack from places the enemy thought were safe.

  • Tier 1–2: Difficult terrain does not reduce your speed while you move toward an attack
  • Tier 3–4: You may move through hostile creature spaces during your turn, though you cannot end there
  • Tier 5: The first reaction used against your movement each round suffers disadvantage or a penalty equal to PS

Ambush

You are strongest at the first true opening.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB on the first attack you make each round against a creature that has not targeted you yet this round
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: That first hit each round deals bonus damage equal to PS and reduces the target’s next defensive contest by PS

Mirage

Enemy precision becomes less reliable against you.

  • Tier 1–2: The first called shot against you each round takes a penalty equal to PB
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If that called shot fails, the attacker loses its bonus action or 10 feet of movement, attacker’s choice

Opportunist

You capitalize when the enemy has already been opened.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB to attacks against creatures that are airborne, prone, rattled, or otherwise compromised
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: Against compromised targets, your attacks gain +PS damage and ignore one layer of defensive mitigation as determined by the table’s normal rules.

Medium Armor Properties

Stable defense, reaction support, anti-pressure tools, and keeping yourself and a nearby ally composed.

Brace

You do not take the full force of a clean hit.

  • Tier 1–2: Reduce damage from the first attack that hits you each round by PB
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce it by PS
  • Tier 5: If that hit is a heavy hit, reduce its damage by 20%, then gain Bonus ADR equal to PS until the start of your next turn

Read Through

You see tricks for what they are.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB to Reads against Feints and reaction baits
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead, and ignore the -2 from Read Fatigue
  • Tier 5: The first Feint used against you each round automatically fails unless the attacker exceeds your defense by PS or more, and that attacker takes a penalty equal to PS on its next attack

Counterweight

You are hard to throw out of the exchange.

  • Tier 1–2: Reduce knockback and forced movement by PB rounded up
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce it by PS.
  • Tier 5: If forced movement is reduced to 0, gain +PS on your next Block, Deflect, or Dodge before the end of your next turn

Tempered

The second hit is never as good as the first.

  • Tier 1–2: The second and later hits against you each round are reduced by PB or 10%, whichever is better
  • Tier 3–4: The second and later hits against you each round are reduced by PS or 15%, whichever is better
  • Tier 5: The second and later hits against you each round deal 20% less damage, and each additional hit grants Bonus ADR equal to PS ÷ 2 until the start of your next turn, up to PS

Catch

You stop one nearby ally from getting opened up for free.

  • Tier 1–2: When an ally within 5 feet is hit, reduce that damage by PB
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce it by PS
  • Tier 5: That ally also gains +PS AC against the next attack made against it before the start of your next turn

Recenter

A successful defense lets you recover your structure.

  • Tier 1–2: When you succeed on a saving throw against an attack, effect, or Disable, gain temp HP equal to PB
  • Tier 3–4: Gain temp HP equal to PS
  • Tier 5: Also remove one temporary movement penalty, attack penalty, or similar debuff

Deflect Line

You defend with good form, not desperation.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB to Block
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: After a successful Block or Deflect, gain +PS AC until the start of your next turn and +PS against the next called shot made against you in that time

Surefooted

You stay upright, armed, and useful.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB to resist prone, shove, and disarm effects
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: The first time each round you would be knocked prone, disarmed, or lose your footing, ignore it

Interfere

You make nearby attacks less clean.

  • Tier 1–2: When a creature within 5 feet attacks an ally, that attack takes a penalty equal to PB
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: If the attack misses, you may immediately move 5 feet or make one basic attack against the attacker

Hold Fast

Your defense improves when you commit to your stance.

  • Tier 1–2: If you move no more than 10 feet this turn, gain +PB AC until the start of your next turn
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: Also gain Bonus ADR equal to PS

Layered Guard

Precision attacks do not unravel you as easily.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB against called shots, limb-targeting, and Disable attempts
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: The first successful called shot against you each round has its duration halved or its effect downgraded, and any Disable duration applied to you is reduced by 50%

Backswing

A failed defense does not mean the exchange is lost.

  • Tier 1–2: The first time each round your Block, Deflect, or Dodge fails, reduce the resulting damage by PB
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce it by PS
  • Tier 5: Gain +PS to your next defensive contest before the end of your next turn, and the attacker takes a penalty equal to PS on its next attack

Heavy Armor Properties

For tanks and frontliners who hold space by force. Heavy does not just survive. It denies.

Juggernaut

The things meant to crack tanks work worse on you.

  • Tier 1–2: Reduce percent-based damage, max HP damage, and takedown damage by 5%
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce them by 10%
  • Tier 5: Reduce them by 15%, and when this reduction applies gain Bonus ADR equal to PS until the start of your next turn

Anchor

You are not where the enemy wants you.

  • Tier 1–2: Reduce knockback and forced movement by PB × 5 feet
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce it by PS × 5 feet
  • Tier 5: Once per round, if forced movement or launch would still apply, you may ignore it entirely

Fortress

Burst does not land cleanly against you.

  • Tier 1–2: Reduce critical bonus damage by 25%
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce critical bonus damage by 50%
  • Tier 5: Critical hits against you become normal hits unless the attacker exceeds your AC by 10 or more

Last Stand

The closer you are to falling, the harder you are to finish.

  • Tier 1–2: While below half HP, reduce all incoming damage by 10%
  • Tier 3–4: While below half HP, reduce all incoming damage by 15%
  • Tier 5: While below half HP, reduce all incoming damage by 20% and gain resistance to forced movement, disarm, and Disable effects

Lockdown

Getting close to you costs movement and freedom.

  • Tier 1–2: When you hit a creature, reduce its speed by PB.
  • Tier 3–4: Reduce it by PS
  • Tier 5: If its speed is reduced to 0 this way, it also loses a reaction

Suppress

Enemies near you do not execute cleanly.

  • Tier 1–2: Enemies within 5 feet take a penalty equal to PB on attack rolls
  • Tier 3–4: Penalty becomes PS
  • Tier 5: Those enemies also deal reduced damage equal to PS

Phalanx

Your armor protects the line, not just yourself.

  • Tier 1–2: Allies within 5 feet gain +PB AC against ranged attacks, opportunity attacks, and movement-triggered reactions
  • Tier 3–4: Use PS instead
  • Tier 5: The first time an ally within 5 feet would be knocked back, launched, or forced prone each round, reduce that effect by PS.

Intercept

If they try to attack through you, they pay for it.

  • Tier 1–2: When a creature within 5 feet attacks an ally, that attack takes a penalty equal to PB
  • Tier 3–4: Penalty becomes PS
  • Tier 5: If that attack misses, you may immediately make one basic attack against that creature

Bastion

Standing your ground makes you even harder to break.

  • Tier 1–2: If you move no more than 5 feet this turn, gain +PB AC until the start of your next turn
  • Tier 3–4: Gain +PS AC instead
  • Tier 5: Also gain Bonus ADR equal to PS

Bulwark

You refuse to become an easy finish.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB against becoming prone, airborne, Vulnerable, or otherwise takedown-eligible
  • Tier 3–4: Gain +PS instead
  • Tier 5: Once per round, if you would become takedown-eligible, you may immediately end one qualifying condition, and takedown damage against you is reduced by 15%

Platebreak Resist

You are harder to dismantle with precision and call shots.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB against called shots, limb-targeting, and precision-based Disable attempts
  • Tier 3–4: Gain +PS instead
  • Tier 5: The first successful called shot against you each round is downgraded, shortened, or negated if the attack did not exceed your AC by at least PS

Weight

Your mass affects the fight before your weapon does.

  • Tier 1–2: Enemies within 5 feet take a penalty equal to PB on Dynamic Reaction checks
  • Tier 3–4: Penalty becomes PS
  • Tier 5: The first Dynamic Reaction used by an adjacent enemy each round is made with disadvantage or a penalty equal to PS.

Iron Grip

You turn control attempts into control of your own.

  • Tier 1–2: Gain +PB on shove and grapple attempts
  • Tier 3–4: Gain +PS instead
  • Tier 5: If you succeed on a shove or grapple, the target also loses 1 Momentum stack or takes damage equal to PS

Legendary Light

The next attack made against that creature before the end of your next turn gains advantage, gains bonus damage equal to PS, and causes the creature to lose all Momentum or 1 Dynamic Reaction, your choice.

Second Nature. The first time each round you are hit by an attack, targeted by a called shot, or targeted by a Feint, you may immediately choose one of the following benefits until the start of your next turn: gain Bonus ADR equal to PS, gain +PS to Block, Deflect, and Dodge contests, or gain +PS against called shots, Feints, and Disable effects. Transformations

Every transformation takes a toll. Your Transformation Limit (TL) is the most divine power you can safely control: think of it as a glass. It's based on your stats, your relationship with your god, and the abilities your god grants.

Max TL = (CON + one other stat) × 10 + bonus (if applicable). The second stat and bonus vary by character. Current and max TL are tracked on your sheet.

Battlefield Systems

Systems exclusive to Mythic Covenant that shape larger fights: monsters with barriers, battalions and their gambits, the morale of every unit on the field, and one very risky god.

Fighting Monsters

Innate Counter

Whenever a monster is attacked, it counterattacks for free (no reaction) after the attack. Excludes gambits and attacks from outside its range. A unit targeted by the counter may only Dodge, and that Dodge costs no reaction.

Barrier

Intact 50% On
Cracked 25% On

  • Some entities vary (up to 75% reduction).
  • A barrier breaks instantly if the monster is vulnerable to the damage type. Effective weapon hits also break one barrier.
  • AoE can only crack barriers. Gambits break the primary target's barrier and crack the others.
  • Barriers drop only one stage per attack chain.
  • Once broken, Innate Counter is offline for one round or until the creature is attacked again.

Barrier Break (all barriers destroyed): the creature is Stunned for one round, can't regenerate barriers, and grants one monster ore or rare material.

Common Barrier Abilities: Vital Defense (nullifies critical hits), Anti-Magic Defense (nullifies magical attacks and effects), Evasive Guard (+10 to dodge against a chosen damage type).

Battalions & Gambits

Characters can purchase battalions to unleash special tactics. Battalions are real people: they can be trained or improved with money and only join battles where they make sense in the fiction (monster hunts, training with soldiers, etc.).

Gambit types: Physical (bludgeoning, slashing, piercing), Magical (a magical attack, usually spell-based), Support (magical healing, AC, or spell saves).

Battalion Stat Block

  • Endurance: the battalion's cohesion ("HP"). Drops by 1 each time the commander is struck by an attack, and by the gambit's cost when one is used.
  • Gambit Charges: how many times the gambit can fire before it must Replenish.
  • Deploy Bonus: while deployed (equipped), the commander gains a passive bonus to hit, AC, and one save.
  • Gambit(s): the tactic itself, with a range and area.

Gambit Rules

  • Charges & Replenish: baseline one use per combat, raised by Team Combat or leadership RP. The Replenish mechanic may change.
  • Endurance & Routing: at 0 Endurance the battalion is Routed: no Deploy Bonus and no gambits until repaired (spend gold between sessions, or recover 1 Endurance per day).
  • No counterplay: a creature hit by a gambit can't react to it or counterattack. This overrides Innate Counter.
  • Action economy: offensive gambits cost an Action; support gambits cost a Bonus Action.
  • Rattled: every offensive gambit that hits applies Rattled (see Status Effects).
  • Gambit Boost: for each allied unit within 5 ft of the commander when the gambit fires, it gains accuracy and an extra target or tile.
  • Accuracy: offensive gambits roll d20 + the battalion's Hit vs. AC. Support gambits force a save vs. DC 8 + commander's CHA + battalion tier, unless stated otherwise.
  • Barriers: breaks the primary target's barrier, cracks the others.

Morale System

Morale measures a creature's willingness to fight. High morale fights boldly; low morale wants to flee. The old system snowballed, so swings are now earned.

1. Swing Cap: a creature can lose at most 30 Morale per round from other creatures' effects. Self-inflicted costs don't count.

2. Tier Lock: a creature can't drop more than one tier per round.

3. Drift: at the end of each round, a creature whose Morale didn't change moves 10 toward 100 (Steady).

4. Commanders: bosses, commanders, and elites can't fall below Shaken. The first time they would hit Broken, they're Staggered instead.

Elemental Statuses

Fire, cold, water, lightning, acid, poison, wind, earth, and radiant.

Burn (Fire)

Fire injury that worsens by degree. Tier I can be removed with an action or a Dynamic Reaction to pat out the flames; allies within 10 ft may assist.

TIER I First Degree Burn

  • Trigger: fire damage ≥ 25% of max HP.
  • 1d6 fire damage at the start of the turn.
  • Disadvantage on the next weapon attack and on Charisma checks.
  • Duration: until extinguished (action or Dynamic Reaction) or healed by a magical source.
  • Worsens into Second Degree if more fire damage is taken within 2 rounds.

TIER II Second Degree Burn

  • Trigger: fire damage ≥ 30% of max HP, or stacking fire effects.
  • Disadvantage on DEX saving throws and checks. Speed halved.
  • Flame persists: 1d6 fire per turn unless an action extinguishes it.
  • Fire vulnerability applies.
  • Duration: until a full action (or Dynamic Reaction) extinguishes it, or magical healing.

TIER III Third Degree Burn

  • Trigger: fire damage ≥ 50% of max HP.
  • Skin chars black: CON save or fall Unconscious for 1 hour.
  • 3d10 fire damage per round. Speed halved; all actions also cost a bonus action.
  • Fire immunity/resistance only halves this damage if applied after the burn.
  • Vulnerable to Cold and Psychic damage (nerve exposure).

TIER IV Cauterized Core

  • Trigger: a Tier III burn + fire damage from a critical hit.
  • 6d10 fire damage per round. Immune to healing for 3 turns.
  • Glows red-hot: any melee attacker takes 2d8 fire.
  • DEX saves at disadvantage.
  • Ends early only by magical water or complete submersion.

Chill (Ice)

TIER I Chilled

  • Trigger: cold damage ≥ 25% of max HP.
  • Speed −10 ft. −4 AC.
  • Only an action or a bonus action (not both). Loses one attack from multiattack.
  • Duration: 1 minute or until warmed by fire or a spell.
  • More cold damage, or receiving this while Soaked, worsens it to Frozen.

TIER II Frozen

  • Trigger: a Chilled creature takes cold again, or a Soaked creature takes cold.
  • Frozen in place (Restrained): can't move or use reactions.
  • Melee attacks against it have advantage; it takes double bludgeoning damage.
  • Fire damage removes Frozen but deals an extra 2d6 thermal shock.
  • Duration: until fire or heat is applied; auto-ends after 3 rounds in a warm climate.

TIER III Frozen Solid

  • Trigger: prolonged ice exposure or failing multiple saves while Chilled.
  • Encased in ice: Incapacitated and Stunned.
  • Resistance to slashing; vulnerability to bludgeoning and fire.

TIER IV Cryoshock

  • Trigger: Frozen Solid + another cold source, or a failed CON save.
  • Speed 0. All damage taken becomes critical hits.
  • Gains 2 levels of Exhaustion over 2 turns.
  • 1d10 cold damage per turn internally (ignores resistance).
  • Fire or lightning damage of 20+ ends it early.

Soaked (Water)

Water primes a creature for cold and lightning combos.

TIER I Soaked

  • Resistance to fire. Vulnerability to cold and lightning.
  • Disadvantage on DEX saves and Acrobatics; advantage on Grapple checks.
  • Cold while Soaked immediately triggers Chilled or Frozen. Lightning while Soaked triggers Shocked and amplifies the damage.

TIER II Drenched

  • Trigger: Soaked + cold, lightning, or frost damage in combination.
  • Automatically Soaked. Lightning or cold deals 1.5× damage.
  • Disadvantage on Initiative and Stealth.
  • STR-based attacks deal half damage (water resistance).
  • Duration: 3 rounds unless dried or ignited.

TIER IV Internal Drowning

  • Trigger: Soaked or Drenched + forced inhalation of magical or tainted water.
  • 5d10 bludgeoning damage from water pressure.
  • Can't speak or cast verbal spells.
  • Grappled by its own lungs: CON save each turn or fall Unconscious.
  • Treated as underwater, even on dry land.

Shock (Lightning)

TIER I Shocked

  • Speed halved. −5 to STR checks and melee attacks (stacks to −10).
  • Lightning damage gains +10 flat per damage die (e.g. 8d6 + 80).

  • Contagion: touching other creatures transfers Shock; metal weapons, armor, and structures carry it.

TIER II Overloaded

  • Trigger: more lightning damage while Shocked.
  • −10 to STR checks, saves, and melee attacks.
  • Speed 0 unless the creature passes a DC 16 CON save at the start of its turn.
  • Each metallic item worn or held deals 1d10 lightning per turn.
  • Touching another creature forces a DC 14 DEX save or it becomes Shocked.
  • Duration: 2 rounds (+2 if Soaked or in water).

TIER III Supercharged

  • Trigger: over 40 lightning damage in one round while Shocked or Soaked.
  • Deals 3d12 lightning to every creature within 5 ft at the start of its turn; touching or grappling it means automatic Shocked.
  • Takes 2d12 lightning per turn; can't Dash or Disengage.
  • On death, explodes in a 15 ft radius for 5d12 lightning.

TIER IV Overcharged

  • Trigger: Tier III Shock while Soaked or wearing metal.
  • 4d12 lightning per round.
  • At the start of its turn, creatures within 5 ft make a DEX save or take 3d8.
  • Can't take bonus actions (neural overload).
  • On death, detonates for 6d10 lightning in a 10 ft radius.

Corrosion (Acid)

TIER I Corrosive

TIER III Acid Meltdown

  • Gains Weakened and Hemorrhage.
  • 4d10 acid per turn; DC 20 CON save each turn or fall Prone.

TIER IV Acid Collapse

  • Trigger: Tier III Corrosion for 3+ turns, or failing 2 STR saves in a row.

  • 5d10 acid per round.

Limb Injury

Bones, joints, and limbs, from sprains to amputation.

TIER I Sprained Joint

TIER I Dislocated Shoulder

  • From being slammed, thrown, or a massive upper-body strike.

TIER II Broken Limb

  • The limb is unusable.
  • Leg: speed halved; no Dash or jump. Arm: drop held items, can't attack or perform somatic components with it.
  • Requires magical healing, or splinting and rest.

TIER II Dislocated Joint

  • A shoulder, hip, or jaw comes out.
  • −5 to attacks with that limb; can't use that arm or leg; double stamina loss if applicable.

TIER II Compound Fracture

  • The bone breaks through the skin.
  • 1d10 Bleed per turn and −4 to any physical check or save.
  • Also causes Weakened and Pain Shock.

TIER III Bone Shatter

  • Trigger: bludgeoning over 100 damage in a single hit.

TIER III Amputated Limb

  • Trigger: a critical hit to an injured limb, or a slashing called shot.
  • The limb is removed: 4d12 bleeding damage immediately and Hemorrhage.

  • Cure: high-level magic (Regenerate, Wish) or surgical prosthetics. May permanently change the character's fighting style.

TIER IV Skeletal Collapse

TIER IV Multilimb Amputation

  • Gains Bleeding, Weakened, and Imbalanced; 2d6 damage per round until stabilized.

TIER IV Nerve Death

  • A limb or region is necrotized or numb beyond recovery.
  • Attacks with that limb have disadvantage and deal half damage. Pain-based abilities can't target it.

Metaphysical Statuses

Soul Erosion

Soul Erosion is now a damage type: Necrotic + Psychic. Plain necrotic damage no longer erodes the soul; it must be the fusion of the two. When a creature takes Soul Erosion damage, it ignores temp HP, resistance to necrotic, psychic, and nonmagical sources doesn't apply, and conventional damage-type mitigation can't reduce it. It isn't unstoppable: explicit immunity or resistance to soul effects still applies; artifacts and divine protections work normally; undead, celestials, fiends, and other spiritually potent beings may have special protection at the DM's discretion. Tentative, may change again.

Status Mixing

When a creature has two or more statuses of different categories or elements, they can combine into a hybrid.

  • Most mixes happen automatically; rare ones may need an Arcana, Medicine, or Insight check to control.
  • The hybrid's tier equals the higher tier involved.
  • A creature can suffer only one mixed effect at a time; applying another replaces the old one.
  • The DM may apply escalation effects: combos left unresolved can evolve into Tier IV ailments or permanent impairments.

Amor Fati (Synced)

You are the ocean's gray waves, choose thy destiny. Anchor: ??? (Favorable) & two of the users. Cooldown: twice per combat.

  • Loryana — Present (Reaction): temporarily stop time to move up to 30 ft. No effects resolve until the movement ends.

Choir of the Godless Pantheon
